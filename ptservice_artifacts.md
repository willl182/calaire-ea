Yes — you mean: **the technical scheme is basically solved, but the “service/business/system” around it is still blurry**. For a PT provider, “building the service” is not only “how do I calculate z-scores?” It is: **who buys it, why they trust it, how they enroll, how rounds run, how evidence is controlled, how reports are delivered, and how the provider becomes credible/accreditable.**

Your uploaded ISO/IEC 17043 material already shows that a PT provider must prove competence, impartiality, consistent operation, controlled scheme design, communication, confidentiality, reports, complaints, appeals, records, risks, audits, and management review — so the missing piece is turning that into a **service operating model**.  ISO/IEC 17025 matters where your own or outsourced measurements support characterization, homogeneity, stability, calibration, traceability, uncertainty, or equipment verification.  ISO 13528 is your statistical backbone for assigned value, `sigma_pt`, uncertainty, robust stats, scores, homogeneity/stability, and reporting. 

## 1. Think of it as 5 products, not 1 service

A PT scheme service has five “layers”:

| Layer                           | What you need to build                                                                | Example for your case                               |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Technical PT scheme**         | Rounds, items, assigned value, homogeneity/stability, scores                          | Air quality gas / pollutant PT rounds               |
| **Quality system**              | ISO/IEC 17043 procedures, records, impartiality, confidentiality, complaints, appeals | P-PSEA, F-PSEA, DG-PSEA documents                   |
| **Customer experience**         | Enrollment, instructions, portal, deadlines, support, certificates/reports            | Participant guide, registration form, result upload |
| **Commercial model**            | Pricing, schedule, scope, contracts, market positioning                               | Annual subscription or per-round fee                |
| **Trust/accreditation pathway** | Evidence for accreditation body, recognized scope, external credibility               | ISO/IEC 17043 accreditation later                   |

ISO/IEC 17043:2023 is explicitly the standard for competence, impartiality, and consistent operation of PT providers. ([ISO][1]) ILAC has also stated that accreditation to ISO/IEC 17043:2010 stops being recognized after the transition period to the 2023 version, so your system should be built directly around **ISO/IEC 17043:2023**, not the old version. ([ILAC – ILAC Live Site][2])

## 2. Research in 6 tracks

### Track A — Market demand

You are not only asking “can I offer PT?” You need to ask:

Who is forced or motivated to participate in PT?

For environmental/air-quality laboratories, likely buyers may include:

| Segment                                  | Why they buy PT                                               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ISO/IEC 17025 accredited labs            | Need external quality assurance and PT participation evidence |
| Regulatory monitoring networks           | Need comparability and confidence in measurements             |
| Universities/research labs               | Need external validation                                      |
| Calibration/testing service providers    | Need competence evidence                                      |
| Government or private environmental labs | Need periodic performance checks                              |

ILAC P9:01/2024 is relevant here because it sets policy for accreditation bodies on the use of proficiency testing and interlaboratory comparisons in accreditation processes. ([ILAC – ILAC Live Site][3]) That means your commercial argument is not just “we offer PT”; it is: **we help accredited or accreditation-seeking laboratories satisfy external performance-monitoring expectations.**

### Track D — Service design / customer journey

This is probably the part you mean by “build the service.”

A participant journey should look like this:

1. **Announcement / round calendar**
2. **Participant registration**
3. **Contract / terms / confidentiality**
4. **Participant code assignment**
5. **Instructions issued**
6. **PT item prepared / dispatched / generated**
7. **Participant measures and reports**
8. **Data validation and technical review**
9. **Statistical analysis**
10. **Draft report / final report**
11. **Appeals / complaints window**
12. **Corrective-action support or interpretation guidance**
13. **Next-round enrollment**

For ISO/IEC 17043, the scheme communication must include objectives, participation criteria, assigned-value and performance-evaluation criteria, confidentiality, schedule, fees, application instructions, and changes to design/operation. That matches your own 17043 requirement file. 

So you need “service artifacts,” not only procedures:

| Artifact                       | Purpose                              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Public service page            | Explain what the PT scheme is        |
| Scheme catalogue               | Scope, analytes, matrices, frequency |
| Round calendar                 | Dates and deadlines                  |
| Participant agreement          | Legal/commercial basis               |
| Confidentiality policy         | Protect identities/results           |
| Participant instructions       | Measurement/reporting rules          |
| Result submission template/app | Reduce unit/transcription errors     |
| Report template                | Deliver standardized evidence        |
| FAQ / interpretation guide     | Help labs understand scores          |
| Complaint/appeal form          | Required by ISO/IEC 17043            |
| Terms of use for reports       | Avoid misleading claims              |

### Track E — Statistical/product policy

Even if the stats are technically in place, you need product-level decisions:

| Decision                              | Example                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is the assigned value strategy?  | Reference gas, CRM, expert lab, consensus                      |
| What score will be primary?           | z-score, z′, zeta, En                                          |
| What is `sigma_pt` based on?          | Regulatory limit, historical reproducibility, expert criterion |
| What happens with few participants?   | Issue limited report, no score, use independent value          |
| How are methods grouped?              | Same value for all or method-specific values                   |
| What is reported publicly?            | Aggregated results only                                        |
| What is the appeal window?            | e.g. 10 business days                                          |
| What is the report correction policy? | Amendment procedure                                            |

ISO 13528:2022 is specifically intended for statistical methods used by PT providers to design schemes and analyze PT data, including interpretation by participants and accreditation bodies. ([Austrian Standards][6]) Your internal ISO 13528 file already maps the key evidence: plan, assigned value, uncertainty, `sigma_pt`, scoring rules, homogeneity/stability, raw participant data, review records, calculations, software validation, and final report.

## 3. Minimum “build plan” for your PT service

I would structure your work like this:

### Phase 1 — Define the offer

Write a one-page service definition:

```text
Service name:
PT scheme for [pollutant/matrix/instrument/method]

Target participants:
[types of laboratories or monitoring organizations]

Purpose:
Evaluate participant performance for [measurement] using [assigned value strategy] and [performance score].

Scope:
Analytes:
Matrices/items:
Measurement range:
Frequency:
Report type:
Accreditation status:
```

### Phase 2 — Build the public-facing package

Create:

1. service webpage or PDF brochure
2. round calendar
3. participant guide
4. registration form
5. pricing table
6. confidentiality and terms document
7. report example with dummy data

This is what turns “technical capability” into a service.

### Phase 3 — Build the operational workflow

Use a round master checklist:

| Stage                         | Evidence                     |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Round approved                | Ficha básica / plan de ronda |
| Participants accepted         | Registration records         |
| Items prepared                | Item control record          |
| Homogeneity/stability checked | H/E record                   |
| Instructions sent             | Communication record         |
| Results received              | Raw data log                 |
| Data reviewed                 | Preprocessing record         |
| Analysis performed            | Statistical output           |
| Report reviewed               | Technical approval           |
| Report issued                 | Final report                 |
| Feedback/appeals handled      | Complaint/appeal records     |

### Phase 4 — Run a pilot round

The pilot round should be treated as if it were accredited, even if not accredited yet. This gives you:

* evidence of feasibility;
* real participant feedback;
* real timing and cost data;
* proof of software/report workflow;
* weaknesses to correct before formal accreditation.

### Phase 5 — Prepare accreditation evidence

Before applying, prepare a gap matrix:

| ISO/IEC 17043 clause | Procedure | Format/record | Evidence from pilot | Gap |
| -------------------- | --------- | ------------- | ------------------- | --- |

Your uploaded requirement documents already have the right skeleton for this.

## 5. The practical deliverables you probably need first

I would not start with more procedures. I would start with these 10 documents/assets:

2. **Scope of PT scheme** — analytes, matrices, levels, methods.
3. **Participant guide** — how labs participate.

## 6. The core strategic question

The big decision is this:

**Are you first launching a non-accredited pilot PT service to generate evidence, participants, and process maturity — or are you trying to go directly to ISO/IEC 17043 accreditation?**

My recommendation: **pilot first, but build it as if it were accredited.** That lets you validate demand, pricing, logistics, reports, statistics, and participant communication before spending too much on formal accreditation.

A good starting positioning could be:

> “A specialized proficiency testing scheme for air-quality measurement laboratories and monitoring organizations, designed under ISO/IEC 17043:2023 principles and ISO 13528 statistical methods, with a roadmap toward formal accreditation.”

That wording is careful: it does **not** falsely claim accreditation, but it signals serious design.
