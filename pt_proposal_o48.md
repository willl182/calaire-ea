# Proposal for the CALAIRE-EA Gaseous Air Pollutants Proficiency Testing Service

**Proposed service:** CALAIRE-EA Proficiency Testing Scheme for Gaseous Air Pollutants  
**Components:** CO, SO₂, NO, NO₂, and optional O₃  
**Service model:** Controlled-atmosphere intercomparison for ambient air quality monitoring instruments  
**Benchmark basis:** Umweltbundesamt / Environment Agency Austria gaseous air pollutant PT model  
**Normative basis:** ISO/IEC 17043:2023, ISO 13528:2022, and relevant ISO/IEC 17025 controls for reference measurements, calibration, traceability, uncertainty, and equipment verification  
**Status:** Proposal for service launch, documentation, commercial model, and accreditation-readiness

---

## 1. Executive summary

CALAIRE-EA proposes to establish a specialized proficiency testing (PT) service for gaseous air pollutants measured in ambient air quality monitoring. The service will evaluate the performance of participant instruments and measurement systems for carbon monoxide (CO), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), nitric oxide (NO), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), and, when technically available, ozone (O₃).

The proposed service is designed as a controlled-atmosphere intercomparison. Participants will connect their instruments to a controlled gas generation and distribution system, measure a sequence of zero and concentration levels, submit their results, and receive a statistically evaluated performance report. The service is benchmarked against the Umweltbundesamt / Environment Agency Austria model, which operates accredited proficiency testing for gaseous air pollutants using a facility-based intercomparison approach with controlled concentrations, reference values, uncertainty-aware scoring, and structured reporting.

The strategic objective is to convert CALAIRE-EA’s technical capability and pilot experience into a repeatable, sellable, and accreditation-ready PT service. The proposal focuses on the two gaps previously identified: **service artifacts** and **commercial model**. It therefore defines not only the technical design, but also the participant journey, public offer, registration documents, terms, confidentiality policy, reporting package, pricing architecture, cancellation policy, and implementation roadmap.

The recommended launch approach is a **paid pre-accreditation round**, clearly positioned as designed under ISO/IEC 17043:2023 and ISO 13528 principles, without claiming accreditation until formal accreditation is granted. The round should be operated using the same documentation discipline expected from an accredited provider so that records from the launch round can support future accreditation evidence.

## 2. Purpose of the proposal

This proposal establishes the complete service concept for a CALAIRE-EA proficiency testing scheme for gaseous air pollutants. It is intended to support:

1. Internal approval of the service model.
2. Preparation of customer-facing service artifacts.
3. Definition of the commercial and pricing architecture.
4. Alignment of the service with ISO/IEC 17043:2023 and ISO 13528:2022 principles.
5. Preparation for future accreditation under ISO/IEC 17043.
6. Launch of a paid controlled-atmosphere PT round following the completed pilot.

The proposal is not a replacement for the official standards. It is a practical implementation document that translates benchmark findings and quality-system requirements into a service launch plan.

## 3. Benchmark basis and rationale

The Umweltbundesamt benchmark is directly relevant because it addresses the same technical family of proficiency testing: gaseous air pollutants measured by ambient air monitoring instruments. Its model provides useful lessons for CALAIRE-EA in five areas:

| Benchmark aspect | Relevance for CALAIRE-EA |
|---|---|
| Target market | Confirms that participants are not limited to laboratories; monitoring networks, authorities, manufacturers, consultants, and research institutes are also buyers. |
| Facility model | Demonstrates that gas PT can be operated as a controlled intercomparison facility rather than by shipping samples. |
| Published scope and ranges | Provides a model for presenting components, concentration ranges, methods, and evaluation criteria in a scheme catalogue. |
| Pricing model | Shows that gas PT is a premium technical assurance service, benchmarked at €1,655 excluding VAT for a full gas PT package including O₃, SO₂, CO, and NO/NO₂. |
| Administrative model | Provides examples of annual programme, order form, cancellation policy, complaint/appeal windows, reporting, confidentiality, and accreditation positioning. |

The benchmark also strengthens the technical design by showing detailed operational features such as assigned workplaces, ring-line distribution, zero-gas bracketing, concentration sequences, stabilization periods, participant questionnaires, z′/En scoring, and reference-value validation. The benchmark's a1–a7 aggregate grading is **not** adopted: CALAIRE-EA reports each score separately (section 10.7).

## 9. Technical operating concept

### 9.1 Facility and connection model

Each participant instrument is assigned a fixed measurement position or connection point. The system should prevent cross-contamination, pressure instability, flow imbalance, or participant-to-participant interference.

Minimum requirements:

- assigned participant positions;
- connection instructions;
- leak checks where applicable;
- controlled gas distribution system;
- documented flow and pressure conditions;
- zero-gas and span-gas generation records;
- event log for interruptions, instrument alarms, or connection issues;
- environmental condition monitoring if relevant.

### 9.2 Gas concentration sequence and run times

Per component, the sequence executed is one zero level plus four concentration levels drawn from the declared range (section 6.1):

| Series | Level | Run time |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Zero | 1 h |
| 1 | Low | 1 h 30 |
| 2 | Medium-low | 1 h 30 |
| 3 | Medium-high | 1 h 30 |
| 4 | High | 1 h 30 |

Nominal concentrations per level are fixed in the round plan and in the per-analyte procedures, in µmol/mol (CO) or nmol/mol (SO₂, NO, NO₂, O₃).

The 1 h 30 is the **total run time per level**, which contains the stabilization/flushing period and the reporting interval. It is not a stabilization period on top of the measurement, and the benchmark's "1.5–2 h stabilization per level" must not be copied on top of it, or round duration doubles.

A single-gas block therefore runs 1 h + 4 × 1 h 30 = **7 h**. The NO₂ block depends on the alternative selected in 9.2.1.

Two design points remain open and must be closed in `P-PSEA-10` to `P-PSEA-13` before the offer is published:

1. **Closing zero.** The current level tables place the zero only at the start (Series 0). A final zero level is needed if baseline drift over the block is to be evaluated. Either add it to the procedures or drop the drift claim from customer-facing documents.
2. **Replicates.** No repeated levels are currently defined. Repeatability is presently addressed through the homogeneity/stability design (section 9.5), not through replicated participant levels.

#### 9.2.1 NO₂ generation alternatives

NO₂ is generated exclusively by gas-phase titration (GPT) of NO with O₃. `P-PSEA-10` defines two mutually exclusive alternatives. One must be selected per round, in the round plan, before execution.

**Alternative 1 — fixed NO level.** NO is held at a fixed level; O₃ titration is varied. Illustrative values below in nmol/mol, per the levels currently defined in `P-PSEA-10`.

| Series | Level | NO nominal | O₃ titration | Expected NO₂ | Run time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Zero | 180 | – | 0 | 1 h |
| 1 | Low | 180 | 30 | 30 | 1 h 30 |
| 2 | Medium-low | 180 | 75 | 75 | 1 h 30 |
| 3 | Medium-high | 180 | 125 | 125 | 1 h 30 |
| 4 | High | 180 | 150 | 150 | 1 h 30 |

Block duration: 7 h. Simpler and cheaper, but NO is not exercised across its range in the same block.

**Alternative 2 — variable NO level.** NO is stepped, each step run with O₃ off and O₃ on. Illustrative values below in nmol/mol.

| Series | Level | NO nominal | O₃ titration | Expected NO₂ | Run time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Zero | 0 | – | 0 | 1 h |
| 1 | Low | 40 | 0 | 0 | 1 h 30 |
| 2 | Low | 40 | 30 | 30 | 1 h 30 |
| 3 | Medium-low | 80 | 0 | 0 | 1 h 30 |
| 4 | Medium-low | 80 | 60 | 60 | 1 h 30 |
| 5 | Medium-high | 120 | 0 | 0 | 1 h 30 |
| 6 | Medium-high | 120 | 90 | 90 | 1 h 30 |
| 7 | High | 180 | 0 | 0 | 1 h 30 |
| 8 | High | 180 | 120 | 120 | 1 h 30 |

Block duration: 1 h + 8 × 1 h 30 = **13 h**. Covers NO and NO₂ jointly and exposes NO₂ converter efficiency across the NO range, at roughly double the gas consumption and stand time.

The selected alternative drives round length, gas consumption, and therefore price. Alternative 2 is technically stronger; Alternative 1 is the defensible launch option if stand time or gas budget is constrained.

### 9.3 Round duration

Round length must be derived from the blocks above, not assumed. Indicative Core round (CO, SO₂, NO, NO₂ with Alternative 1): 4 × 7 h ≈ 28 h of generation, i.e. **4–5 working days** including setup, participant connection, leak checks, and contingency. With NO₂ Alternative 2, add roughly one day.

### 9.4 Generation and reference methods

The item of proficiency testing is an atmosphere of the criteria pollutant in zero air, generated **dynamically in situ** from a certified reference material (CRM) cylinder using a dynamic calibrator, and delivered to participants through a **glass distribution manifold** chosen for material inertness and sample homogeneity. There is no shipping of test items.

| Component | Generation/reference approach |
|---|---|
| CO | Dynamic dilution of CRM cylinder with zero air. |
| SO₂ | Dynamic dilution of CRM cylinder with zero air. |
| NO | Dynamic dilution of CRM NO mixture with zero air. |
| NO₂ | Gas-phase titration of NO with O₃ (Alternative 1 or 2, section 9.2.1). |
| O₃ | Ozone generator with a level-2 photometer in the traceability chain. |

For each component, the round plan must define:

- source gas or generation method;
- traceability route;
- reference instruments or standards;
- uncertainty components;
- stability checks;
- assigned/reference value calculation;
- criteria for accepting or rejecting a level.

### 9.5 Homogeneity and stability of the test item

Because the item is generated in situ rather than shipped, homogeneity and stability are properties of the generation and distribution line, and they must be demonstrated for every round. This is a mandatory ISO/IEC 17043 demonstration and is already designed into `P-PSEA-06`, `P-PSEA-07`, and the per-analyte procedures. It is evaluated under ISO 13528 sections 5.3 (homogeneity) and 5.4 (stability).

**Homogeneity.** Duplicate analyzers are read at different positions along the sampling manifold. The item is homogeneous when the between-sample standard deviation `s_s`, or the observed absolute differences, fall below the critical homogeneity value `c`, derived from the expected proficiency standard deviation `sigma_pt` for that component and level. A direct-zero test is also performed: the calibrator is connected first directly to the analyzer and then through the manifold, which qualifies the whole calibrator–manifold–analyzer line rather than the calibrator alone.

**Stability.** Mean reference-analyzer values are compared across each concentration run. The level is stable when observed differences stay within the defined thresholds plus their associated uncertainty, per component and level.

Any residual inhomogeneity enters the assigned-value uncertainty budget as `u_hom` (section 10.2).

| Record | Content |
|---|---|
| `F-PSEA-11` | Main homogeneity and stability record for the item. |
| `F-PSEA-11A` | Preprocessed homogeneity data. |
| `F-PSEA-11B` | Preprocessed stability data. |
| `F-PSEA-11C` | Homogeneity results. |
| `F-PSEA-11D` | Stability results. |

Homogeneity and stability must be evaluated and accepted **before** participant performance is interpreted. A level that fails is excluded from scoring under the nonconforming work procedure and may still be reported as non-scored technical information.

## 10. Statistical design and evaluation

### 10.1 Statistical principles

The statistical evaluation must be fit for purpose and documented before the round. It should follow ISO 13528 principles and align with ISO/IEC 17043 requirements for statistical design, assigned values, performance criteria, report content, and participant communication.

The design should define:

- objective of the round;
- components and levels;
- expected number of participants;
- minimum number of participants;
- assigned/reference value strategy;
- uncertainty of assigned/reference value;
- performance criterion (`sigma_pt` or equivalent);
- score types;
- interpretation rules;
- handling of uncertainty;
- handling of late, missing, corrected, or technically invalid results;
- treatment of method differences;
- reporting and confidentiality rules.

### 10.2 Assigned/reference value

The assigned value is **not** the calibrator setpoint. The nominal concentration is only the value set on the dynamic calibrator. The assigned value `x_pt` is the **mean of the CALAIRE-EA reference-analyzer measurements** over the established measurement window for that level, the reference analyzers being calibrated against certified reference materials traceable to international standards.

Participant consensus may be used as a plausibility check (section 10.3) but is never the sole assigned value: participant numbers are small and common bias across a monitoring network is plausible.

The standard uncertainty of the assigned value combines:

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `u_cal` | Calibration gas and dilution system. |
| `u_r` | Short-term repeatability (instrument noise). |
| `u_lof` | Lack of fit (non-linearity). |
| `u_d` | Long-term drift between calibrations. |
| `u_infl` | Environmental influences (temperature, pressure). |
| `u_hom` | Residual inhomogeneity of the test gas (section 9.5). |

The expanded uncertainty uses a coverage factor `k = 2` (approximately 95% confidence).

For each level, CALAIRE-EA should document:

- assigned value `x_pt` and its derivation from the reference analyzers;
- standard uncertainty `u(x_pt)`;
- source of uncertainty components;
- generation records;
- stability evidence;
- validation decision;
- decision to score or exclude the level.

### 10.3 Reference value validation

Before final scoring, each assigned/reference value should be reviewed for plausibility against participant robust statistics, historical data, generation records, and uncertainty information.

Recommended validation statistic:

```text
Validation statistic = |x* - X| / sqrt((1.25 s*)^2 / p + u_X^2)
```

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `X` | Provider reference/generated value |
| `x*` | Robust mean of participant results |
| `s*` | Robust standard deviation of participant results |
| `p` | Number of participant results |
| `u_X` | Standard uncertainty of the reference/generated value |

If the validation statistic reaches or exceeds the defined decision limit, the affected level should not be used for scoring unless a documented technical review justifies its use. The result may still be reported as non-scored technical information.

### 10.4 Proficiency standard deviation (`sigma_pt`)

`sigma_pt` is not left open. It is derived from a linear model of the assigned value, per component:

```text
sigma_pt = a * x_pt + b
```

Where `a` is the slope, `b` the intercept, and `x_pt` the assigned/reference value.

| Gas | `a` | `b` (nmol/mol) |
|---|---|---|
| NO | 0.024 | 1 |
| NO₂ | 0.028 | 1.4 |
| SO₂ | 0.022 | 1 |
| O₃ | 0.020 | 1 |
| CO | 0.024 | 100 |

The intercepts are expressed in nmol/mol for every component, **including CO** (`b = 100 nmol/mol = 0.1 µmol/mol`). CO results are reported in µmol/mol, so the intercept must be converted before `sigma_pt` is evaluated for CO. Applying `b = 100` directly against a CO assigned value in µmol/mol inflates `sigma_pt` by three orders of magnitude and drives every CO z-score to approximately zero — a failure that produces plausible-looking output and no error. The conversion must be explicit in the statistical plan and verified in `pt_app`.

### 10.5 Performance scores

CALAIRE-EA uses four scores. z and z′ are alternatives — one or the other applies at a given level, never both — while ζ and En are reported alongside.

| Score | Role |
|---|---|
| z-score | Primary score when the assigned-value uncertainty is negligible. |
| z′-score | Primary score when the assigned-value uncertainty is not negligible. |
| ζ-score (zeta) | Uncertainty-aware compatibility score using **standard** uncertainties of participant and assigned value. |
| En number | Uncertainty-aware compatibility score using **expanded** uncertainties. |
| % difference | Simple interpretive metric for participants; not a pass/fail criterion. |

Symbols used below: `x_i` participant result, `x_pt` assigned value, `u(x_i)` participant standard uncertainty, `u(x_pt)` standard uncertainty of the assigned value, `U(x_i)` and `U(x_pt)` the corresponding expanded uncertainties.

#### 10.5.1 Choice between z and z′

The choice is made per component and level using the ISO 13528 criterion:

```text
if u(x_pt) > 0.3 * sigma_pt  ->  use z'
else                         ->  use z
```

This decision must be recorded per level in the statistical analysis record, together with the values of `u(x_pt)` and `sigma_pt` that produced it. It is not decided once for the whole round.

```text
z  = (x_i - x_pt) / sigma_pt
z' = (x_i - x_pt) / sqrt(sigma_pt^2 + u(x_pt)^2)
```

#### 10.5.2 ζ-score

```text
zeta = (x_i - x_pt) / sqrt(u(x_i)^2 + u(x_pt)^2)
```

ζ answers a different question from z′: it asks whether the participant's deviation is explained by the participant's **own claimed** uncertainty, not by the scheme's fitness-for-purpose criterion. It therefore requires the participant to report a standard uncertainty, and it is sensitive to under- or over-declared uncertainty. A satisfactory z′ combined with an unsatisfactory ζ indicates the participant's uncertainty claim is too small for its own bias.

#### 10.5.3 En number

```text
En = (x_i - x_pt) / sqrt(U(x_i)^2 + U(x_pt)^2)
```

En uses expanded uncertainties, with `U(x_pt)` computed at `k = 2`. It requires the participant to report both an expanded uncertainty and its coverage factor and coverage probability; results reported at a coverage factor other than `k = 2` must be normalized before use.

ζ and En are not independent: with consistent coverage factors on both sides, `En ≈ zeta / k`. Reporting both is a deliberate choice — ζ preserves resolution for internal review, En is the score participants and assessors recognize. The statistical plan must state that they are two presentations of the same comparison so the final report does not read them as two independent confirmations.

Neither ζ nor En should be applied blindly. Reported participant uncertainties must be reviewed for technical plausibility against the equipment questionnaire (section 11). An inflated uncertainty must not be allowed to turn a poor measurement into a satisfactory En.

### 10.6 Acceptance criteria

| Indicator | Criterion | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| z / z′ | \|z\| ≤ 2 | Satisfactory. About 95% of results are expected in this band. |
| z / z′ | 2 < \|z\| < 3 | Questionable. Expected roughly 1 in 20 times; warrants specific review. |
| z / z′ | \|z\| ≥ 3 | Unsatisfactory. Treated as evidence of an anomaly to be investigated and corrected. |
| ζ | \|ζ\| ≤ 2 | Satisfactory: deviation consistent with the declared standard uncertainties. |
| ζ | 2 < \|ζ\| < 3 | Questionable: possible underestimation of the declared uncertainty. |
| ζ | \|ζ\| ≥ 3 | Unsatisfactory: deviation not explained by the declared uncertainties. |
| En | \|En\| < 1 | Satisfactory: error bars touch or cross the reference value. |
| En | \|En\| ≥ 1 | Unsatisfactory. |

The ζ bands above are the ISO 13528 defaults and are proposed here; unlike the z/z′ and En criteria, which are already fixed in the per-analyte procedures `P-PSEA-10` to `P-PSEA-13`, they must be formally adopted in `P-PSEA-07` before first use.

### 10.7 Combined interpretation

There is no aggregate grade and no component-level percentage pass rule. Performance is reported **per component and per level**, as z or z′, ζ, En, and % difference, each against its own criterion in section 10.6.

The scores answer different questions and are reported together precisely so that a disagreement between them stays visible rather than being collapsed into a single symbol:

- z or z′ asks whether the deviation is acceptable against the scheme's fitness-for-purpose criterion (`sigma_pt`).
- ζ and En ask whether the deviation is explained by the uncertainties declared by the participant and the provider.

A satisfactory z′ with an unsatisfactory ζ or En points to an underestimated participant uncertainty, not to a biased measurement. The reverse points to a bias the participant's uncertainty claim is wide enough to absorb. Both cases warrant comment in the final report; neither is reduced to a pass/fail letter.

### 10.7.1 Graphical presentation

Results are presented as: bar charts of z/z′ against the satisfactory/questionable/unsatisfactory limits; bias plots with error bars for ζ and En (satisfactory where the error bar crosses the reference line); and repeatability/reproducibility plots comparing group limits against the criteria of the applicable European standards.

### 10.8 Treatment of low participant numbers

If the number of participants is below the planned minimum, CALAIRE-EA should not rely solely on same-round consensus statistics. Possible alternatives:

- continue with independent reference values and report limitation;
- postpone or combine with a future round;
- issue non-scored technical comparison only;
- convert the round into a closed comparison with limited claims;
- use expert-defined criteria and historical data where available.

This policy must be communicated before the round.

### 10.9 Data review and exclusions

Before final scoring, CALAIRE-EA should perform an initial review for:

- wrong units;
- impossible values;
- transposition errors;
- missing uncertainty or coverage factor;
- instrument downtime;
- participant method differences;
- values reported after the deadline;
- concentrations where the assigned/reference value failed validation;
- distribution anomalies suggesting technical problems.

Any exclusion, correction, or non-scored result must be documented, justified, and traceable.

## 15. Commercial model

### 15.1 Pricing principle

The service should be priced as a premium technical assurance service, not as a routine laboratory test. The benchmark price for a full gas PT round was €1,655 excluding VAT, including O₃, SO₂, CO, NO/NO₂, evaluation, and report. CALAIRE-EA should not copy this price directly, but should use it as an international reference point and validate local pricing through a cost-plus and break-even model.

### 15.2 Recommended packages

| Package | Includes | Target client |
|---|---|---|
| Core Gas PT Round | CO, SO₂, NO, NO₂; evaluation; report; participation statement. | First launch, most networks/labs. |
| Full Gas PT Round | CO, SO₂, NO, NO₂, O₃; evaluation; report; participation statement. | Mature version when O₃ is ready. |
| Single Component Round | One gas only. | Small clients, technical pilots, or focused needs. |

### 15.4 Cost model

The minimum price should be calculated from the round cost, expected participants, and margin.

```text
Total round cost = fixed cost + variable cost × number of participants
Minimum price per participant = total round cost / expected paying participants
Commercial price = minimum price per participant × margin factor
```

Suggested cost categories:

| Cost category | Examples |
|---|---|

| Gas generation | Certified gases, zero air, consumables, generator operation. |
| Reference measurement | Reference analyzer operation, calibration, verification. |

| Quality/accreditation allocation | QMS maintenance, audits, accreditation costs. |
| Administration | Registration, invoicing, customer support. |

## 18. Risk management

| Risk | Impact | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum participants not reached | Financial loss or invalid statistical approach | Minimum participant policy, postponement clause, independent reference value strategy. |
| Gas generation instability | Invalid concentration level | Stability checks, reference validation, nonconforming work procedure. |
| Participant instrument failure | Missing or incomplete results | Event log, non-scored result policy, participant responsibility clause. |
| Incorrect units or data entry | Wrong scores | Controlled result form, unit dropdowns, data validation checklist. |
| Inflated participant uncertainty | Misleading ζ and En results | Cross-check of declared uncertainty against the equipment questionnaire; z/z′ reported alongside, so bias is visible independently of the uncertainty claim. |
| CO unit error (`sigma_pt` intercept `b = 100 nmol/mol` vs CO results in µmol/mol) | `sigma_pt` inflated 1000×; every CO z-score collapses toward zero, with no visible error | Explicit unit conversion in the statistical plan; validated calculation in `pt_app`. |
| Homogeneity or stability failure on a level | Level not valid for scoring | H/E evaluation before performance interpretation; level exclusion under nonconforming work. |
| Reference value failure | Invalid scoring | Reference value validation and level exclusion policy. |
| Confidentiality breach | Loss of trust/legal issue | Code key control, access restrictions, consent declaration. |
| Accreditation misrepresentation | Legal/reputational risk | Controlled accreditation wording. |
| Late cancellation/no-show | Financial loss | Cancellation policy and payment-before-round rule. |
| Report appeal after finalization | Rework and conflict | Draft report and 14-day appeal window. |

## 20. Proposed launch offer

### 20.1 Offer name

**CALAIRE-EA Core Gas PT Round 2027**

### 20.2 Offer description

A controlled-atmosphere proficiency testing round for ambient air quality monitoring instruments measuring CO (0–20 µmol/mol), SO₂ (0–180 nmol/mol), NO (0–500 nmol/mol), and NO₂ (0–250 nmol/mol, by gas-phase titration). Participants measure five nominal levels per component — one zero plus four concentration levels — generated in situ from certified reference materials and delivered through a glass manifold, using their routine instruments and procedures. Results are evaluated using the z-score or z′-score (selected per level under the ISO 13528 criterion), the ζ-score, the En number where the participant reports expanded uncertainty, and percentage difference. No aggregate performance grade is issued.

### 20.3 Included

- Registration and participant confirmation.
- Participant code assignment.
- Participant guide and instructions.
- Equipment and traceability questionnaire.
- Controlled gas exposure/intercomparison.
- Result submission and data validation.
- Statistical evaluation.

- Final report.
- Participation statement.

### 20.4 Not included

- Participant instrument transport.
- Instrument calibration or repair.
- Corrective-action consulting.
- On-site/mobile deployment unless quoted separately.
- Non-standard analysis not defined in the round plan.
- Regulatory certification of participant equipment.

### 20.5 Commercial conditions

- Minimum participants: to be defined, recommended 8–15.

## 21. Conclusion

CALAIRE-EA has the technical foundation and pilot experience needed to move toward a formal proficiency testing service. The key remaining step is to convert that technical capability into a complete service system: a public offer, participant journey, documentation package, commercial model, and accreditation-ready records.

The Umweltbundesamt benchmark shows that a gas PT service can be positioned as a premium external quality assurance product for monitoring networks, laboratories, public authorities, manufacturers, and consultants. It also shows that the service should be packaged clearly, with defined components, ranges, eligibility, price, cancellation rules, scoring methods, confidentiality rules, and report deliverables.

The recommended next step is to launch a paid pre-accreditation round using the documentation discipline of ISO/IEC 17043 and ISO 13528 from the beginning. This will generate revenue, market proof, customer feedback, and accreditation evidence while avoiding premature accreditation claims.

The essential deliverable is therefore:

> **CALAIRE-EA Proficiency Testing Programme 2027 — Gaseous Air Pollutants**

This programme should include the public-facing service description, round calendar, component scope, participant requirements, commercial conditions, statistical evaluation rules, confidentiality policy, complaint/appeal process, and registration pathway.
